Measure a log sample, then size the deployment.

Every SIEM quote turns on two numbers, bytes per event and events per second. Most of the time both are guessed.

In your browser: give it a log file and it reports the bytes per event, the events per second, the message templates and what a year of it takes to keep.

Step 1 · Measure

What the profiler measures

Give it a sample of the log you plan to collect; 50 to 500 MB is the normal case. It reads the file line by line in a Web Worker, on your machine, and reports what a SIEM is sized on.

01 Bytes per event

Every line is counted, terminator included, and the total reconciled to the byte against the size of the file. The average line is the bytes per event a sizing needs.

02 Message templates

Timestamps, addresses, ids and numbers are masked, then Drain groups the lines into the templates behind them, each with its share and its average size. The largest is the first to filter before you pay to keep it.

03 Events per second, and a year

The timestamps, in any of six common formats, give the rate over the time the sample covers. At your retention and compression that is a day and a year of storage. The profile downloads as JSON, the templates as CSV.

The three largest of the sample's 46 templates, as the profiler shows them. <*> is a word that varies; <ts>, <ip>, <num> and <hex> were masked before clustering.

Step 2 · Size

What the app sizes

Capacity planning is arithmetic done under pressure, usually in a meeting. The app does the arithmetic for the whole deployment, source by source, so the conversation can be about the answer.

01 Estimation

Build a list of the sources you will actually collect from, each with its rate and its event size, measured by you or taken from the 39 built in. Set compression, indexing and retention, and get the storage that list needs, the cluster capacity and the number of data nodes to hold it. The List and Storage tabs.

Measure your own event size first →

02 Conversion

Move between message rates and volumes — per second, hour, day, month or year; bits and bytes; decimal and binary. The unit confusion that causes sizing errors, removed. Type the rate and the event size you measured, and + adds them to the list.

03 Comparison

Put a number in human terms. A gram of DNA holds over eight million Blu-rays; a petabyte is so many years of 4K video. Useful for the slide, and for intuition.
