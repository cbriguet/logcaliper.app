What this log takes to keep

The same arithmetic over a list of sources, with replication and the number of nodes, is the Estimation tab of Logcaliper, the free iOS calculator; the measured event sizes it ships with come from samples like this one.

LinesTemplateShareAvg bytes

How this works. The file is read in chunks of about 8 MB by a Web Worker, so the page stays responsive while it runs. Every line's byte length is counted, terminator included, so the totals reconcile to the byte against the size of the file. Timestamps, addresses, ids and numbers are masked, then Drain, the log parsing algorithm, groups each line with the template it shares at least 40% of its words with and wildcards the words that differ; up to 4,000 templates are kept, and past that the least recently used are folded into Other. The rate is the lines that carry a timestamp over the span from the earliest to the latest, in whichever of six common formats the first 200 lines use. The storage line is the arithmetic of the app's Estimation tab, at the retention and compression you pick. In this browser the worker was loaded from worker.js directly rather than built inside the page; it is the same code, and after a stop that had to shut it down it is loaded again the same way.
